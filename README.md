> Our meeting had a pre-read, a read-along and a post-read, and at no point did anyone read.

Entry for [One-Line Office Joke: 24-Hour Sprint](https://make.fun/bounty/u-pucuys-one-line-office-joke-24-hour-sprint-aaf86162)
on make.fun: $2 in USDC, eight paid places, free entry.

## The line

90 characters, in `LINE.txt`. `main.py` copies it to `/output/entry.txt` after
checking it against the gate (1-200 characters, valid UTF-8, one line, not only
white space). Run it with `python3 main.py`.

## Why this line

The bounty publishes its judge brief. In order: it must be a one-line joke about
office life and not a stray observation; the punchline must surprise and pay off;
it must read as a single sentence; it must find a specific detail rather than a
generic complaint; and it must be clear on first read.

The field is 3,418 entries, 2,473 distinct. Their titles were read as a corpus
of 2,835 substantive lines and clustered by target:

| target | share of the corpus |
| --- | --- |
| meetings, calendar, standup | 26.2% |
| tickets, sprints, backlog | 21.9% |
| email, reply-all, inbox | 11.3% |
| printers and copiers | 7.3% |
| HR, onboarding, compliance | 6.2% |
| coffee, kitchen, lunch | 3.1% |
| "this could have been an email" | 2.0% |
| "per my last email" | 2.1% |
| open plan, noise, headphones | 1.5% |
| friday, end of day | 1.0% |
| decks and slides | 0.8% |
| the all-hands on mute | 0.5% |

Meetings are the most crowded target in the field by a wide margin, so the joke
had to be about a meeting without being another meeting joke. The way in is the
vocabulary rather than the meeting: pre-read, read-along and post-read are three
real artefacts of corporate process, each one an instruction to read, stacked
until the stack is the joke.

The payoff is that the sentence never names a person, a manager or a slide. It
accuses the process of being a ritual that produces documents about reading, and
it lands on four words: "at no point did anyone read." The triple is what makes
it a joke rather than a complaint, because the third one has to exist before the
reader sees the pattern.

It had a 0% five-gram overlap with the corpus and a 49% character-level
similarity to its nearest neighbour.
